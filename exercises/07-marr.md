# 07. Analyze one AI system at three levels

Resource: [Vision · Levels of Analysis](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262514620/vision/)

Week 10 · Suggested effort: 4–6 hours

## Prerequisites

One completed implementation from this library. You can preview the three questions on day one and return for the capstone.

## Read

Use chapter 1, “The Philosophy and the Approach,” especially section 1.2 and “The Three Levels.” Borrow Vision through a library or use an authorized purchased/institutional copy.

- [Publisher-hosted chapter access](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/9780262514620.003.0002)

## Explain and derive

The task a system solves, the representation and procedure it uses, and its physical realization answer distinct questions. A strong explanation connects all three.

`Problem & objective → representations & algorithm → physical implementation`

Derive the central relation in your own notation and state the assumptions.

## Implement

Your notes and a working implementation from modules 01–06. No additional compute is needed.

1. Choose one specific system: for example, your tiny next-token model. Define the actual input, output, and evaluation setting.
2. At the computational level, state the problem, objective, assumptions, and what would count as success.
3. At the algorithmic level, describe representations, transformations, learning, and inference.
4. At the implementation level, document the actual hardware, numerical precision, memory use, and execution constraints.
5. Change one level at a time. Predict what stays invariant and what changes, then test one prediction and write a two-page analysis.

## Acceptance checks

- [ ] The analysis separates an objective from the algorithm that pursues it.
- [ ] Every level includes observable evidence and a limitation.
- [ ] One controlled change connects at least two levels without claiming that artificial and biological systems are equivalent.

## Limits

The framework organizes explanations; it does not by itself prove that a particular algorithm models the brain.
